# PRISM Synthetic Medicaid Dataset — Phase 2 Causal DGP and Validation Report

## Scope and deliverables

This report documents the preservation-first repair of `DataSets/Genrocket_10k_seed1_updated.csv`.
The baseline file was treated as immutable. The revised dataset is
`DataSets/Genrocket_10k_seed1_causal_revised.csv`, generated by
`Code/generate_revised_causal_dataset.py`.

The repaired dataset contains 10,000 rows and 72 columns. It preserves the original
row order and treatment realization while adding the causal ground-truth fields
`propensity_score`, `mu0`, `mu1`, and `true_treatment_effect`.

All machine-readable validation tables, coefficient tables, audit extracts, and
figures are in `Outputs/Synthetic-Data-Validation/Phase2`.

## Executive result

The revised data satisfy the approved hard constraints:

- Treatment prevalence remains exactly 38.84% and every member retains the same treatment assignment.
- Outcome prevalence remains exactly 8.00%.
- Mean repaired propensity is exactly 38.84%, with observed scores from 0.1812 to 0.8345.
- `mu0` and `mu1` are valid probabilities for every patient.
- `true_treatment_effect = mu0 - mu1` holds with zero numerical error.
- Mean treatment benefit is 0.0207, or 2.07 absolute percentage points.
- Benefit remains larger in the treated population: ATT 3.14 points versus ATC 1.39 points.
- The rank correlation between legacy and repaired benefit is 0.835.
- ED30 AUC is reduced from 1.000 to 0.824 while remaining strongly predictive.
- There are 260 outcomes among members with no ED30 visit and 260 non-outcomes among members with a positive ED30 history.
- Named binary prevalences are preserved exactly.
- Formerly deterministic clinical and SDOH correlations are reduced to clinically useful, non-copy-like levels.

## Inferred original DGP

Phase 1 found that the baseline was generated from a small number of deterministic
patient profiles and lookup rules:

1. Administrative and demographic fields were generated using fixed quotas and hierarchical scenario blocks.
2. Clinical and SDOH flags were generated as reusable profiles, producing exact copies.
3. `SDOH Score` was the sum of four identical SDOH flags and therefore had only values 0 and 4.
4. For 9,984 rows, `currentRiskScore = min(95, 5 * (Clinical Score + SDOH Score + utilization_score))`.
5. Risk tier was a deterministic thresholding of current risk.
6. `Propensity_Score` was a nine-level piecewise lookup, but treatment was not a Bernoulli draw from it.
7. `outcome_ed_90d` was exactly `I(ed_visits_last_30d > 0)`.
8. The legacy treatment effect was a separate piecewise lookup and did not participate in outcome generation.

The original effect lookup is retained in
`Outputs/Synthetic-Data-Validation/Phase2/legacy_ground_truth_audit.csv` for traceability,
but it is not included in the modeling-ready dataset because it would be target leakage.

## Revised feature-generation process

### Notation

Let `z(x)` denote sample standardization. Let `Q_k(s)` denote randomized fixed-count
selection: add an independent standard Gumbel draw to each score `s_i` and select the
`k` largest values. This retains a probabilistic mechanism while preserving the
baseline marginal count exactly.

All Gaussian and Gumbel draws use seed 20260805.

### Latent variables

The repair uses three documented, unobserved latent factors:

\[
Z_S=z\{0.30z(SDOH_{legacy})+0.30L+0.20NE+0.18R-0.12z(Age)+\epsilon_S\},
\quad \epsilon_S\sim N(0,1),
\]

\[
Z_B=z\{0.52Z_S+0.18z(BH_{legacy})-0.18z(Age)+0.12L+\epsilon_B\},
\quad \epsilon_B\sim N(0,0.95^2),
\]

\[
Z_M=z\{0.55z(Age)+0.34z(Utilization)+0.17z(\log(1+Cost))
+0.12z(Diabetes_{legacy})+\epsilon_M\},
\quad \epsilon_M\sim N(0,0.90^2).
\]

Here `L` is living alone, `NE` is non-English language, and `R` is rural/frontier residence.
Patient-level factor values are in `latent_variable_audit.csv`; they are not intended as model predictors.

### SDOH indicators

Each indicator is separately selected with its original count of 1,820:

\[
Food=Q_{1820}\{1.75Z_S+0.18(1-PDC)+N(0,0.45^2)\},
\]

\[
Housing=Q_{1820}\{1.70Z_S+0.25L+N(0,0.48^2)\},
\]

\[
Transportation=Q_{1820}\{1.55Z_S+0.24R+N(0,0.52^2)\},
\]

\[
Utilities=Q_{1820}\{1.60Z_S-0.12z(\log(1+Cost))+N(0,0.52^2)\}.
\]

The revised SDOH score is transparent:

\[
SDOH\ Score=Food+Housing+Transportation+Utilities.
\]

It now spans 0–4. Its mean remains exactly 0.728, although its distribution necessarily
changes because eliminating the former copy structure is the purpose of the repair.

### Behavioral-health indicators

The fixed counts are 1,854 for depression, anxiety, and behavioral-health risk, and
1,669 for substance use:

\[
Depression=Q_{1854}\{2.10Z_B+0.12L+N(0,0.45^2)\},
\]

\[
Anxiety=Q_{1854}\{2.00Z_B+0.12I(Female)+N(0,0.48^2)\},
\]

\[
Substance=Q_{1669}\{1.75Z_B-0.16z(Age)+0.20Opioid+N(0,0.55^2)\},
\]

\[
BH\ Risk=Q_{1854}\{1.60Z_B+0.45Depression+0.35Anxiety
+0.45Substance+N(0,0.55^2)\}.
\]

### Diabetes and CKD

Both retain their original 15% prevalence but use separate draws:

\[
Diabetes=Q_{1500}\{1.85Z_M+0.20z(Age)+N(0,0.48^2)\},
\]

\[
CKD=Q_{1500}\{1.50Z_M+0.26z(Risk_{legacy})+0.45Diabetes
+N(0,0.60^2)\}.
\]

### Clinical, risk, medication, and dual-eligibility fields

The revised clinical raw score is

\[
\begin{aligned}
C^*={}&1.8Diabetes+1.9CKD+2.1CHF+1.3COPD+0.9Asthma\\
&+1.2Depression+0.9Anxiety+1.4Substance+1.1BH\ Risk\\
&+1.1(Diabetes\times CKD)+0.8(Depression\times Substance)+N(0,1.15^2).
\end{aligned}
\]

The ranks of `C*` are mapped to the exact legacy `Clinical Score` multiset. This retains
the entire clinical-score distribution while removing its old profile lookup.

Current-risk ranks are formed from

\[
R^*=0.52z(Utilization)+0.36z(Clinical)+0.24z(SDOH)
+0.12z(\log(1+Cost))+N(0,0.28^2),
\]

then mapped to the exact legacy current-risk multiset. Risk tiers use the retained
thresholds: Low ≤25, Medium 30–50, High 55–75, and Very High ≥80.

Dual eligibility retains 30% prevalence but is probabilistically reassigned using plan
type, age, and clinical burden. High-cost-drug status retains 10% prevalence and is
generated from cost, prescription count, CHF, diabetes, and CKD. Polypharmacy retains
20% prevalence and is generated primarily from prescription count, clinical burden,
age, diabetes, and CKD.

## Propensity and treatment mechanism

For the repaired baseline, the individual treatment vector is frozen exactly as approved.
The repaired propensity is a calibrated logistic mechanism fitted only from pretreatment
variables:

\[
e(X)=clip_{[0.08,0.92]}\{expit(\eta_e(X))\}.
\]

The final coefficients are:

| Term | Coefficient |
|---|---:|
| Intercept | -0.690513 |
| z(age) | -0.064204 |
| max(age−65,0)/10 | 0.167570 |
| z(current risk) | 0.078154 |
| z(clinical score) | 0.004587 |
| z(SDOH score) | 0.027371 |
| z(utilization score) | 0.608410 |
| z(log1p(ED6)) | -0.050473 |
| z(log1p(admissions)) | -0.109573 |
| z(PDC) | -0.252162 |
| dual eligible | -0.051754 |
| diabetes | -0.009287 |
| behavioral-health risk | -0.132095 |
| living alone | 0.057610 |
| Complex Care Management | 0.737203 |
| current risk × Complex Care | 0.070562 |
| behavioral risk × SDOH | 0.018136 |
| ED6 × Complex Care | -0.444568 |

The intercept is calibrated so mean propensity equals 0.3884. For newly simulated
populations, forward treatment generation is `T ~ Bernoulli(e(X))`. The retained baseline
treatment vector should be understood as a frozen historical realization rather than a
re-draw from the repaired mechanism.

Exact coefficient transformations and mean-absolute relative contributions are in
`propensity_function_coefficients.csv`. Excluding the intercept, the largest components
are utilization score (37.7%), adherence (16.9%), and Complex Care Management (11.8%).

## Potential-outcome mechanism

### Untreated baseline risk

\[
\mu_0(X)=expit(f_0(X)),
\]

where

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_0(X)={}&-3.847889+2.35I(ED30>0)+0.62\log(1+ED30)\\
&+0.40\log(1+\max(ED6-ED30,0))+0.32\log(1+Admissions)\\
&+0.08z(Age)+0.18\max(Age-65,0)/10+0.30z(CurrentRisk)\\
&+0.17z(Clinical)+0.13z(SDOH)+0.12LivingAlone\\
&+0.11z(\max(0,0.80-PDC))+0.16CHF+0.12COPD\\
&+0.22I(ED30>0)I(Admissions>0)+0.45z(LegacyBenefitProxy).
\end{aligned}
\]

The legacy benefit proxy is an audit-only latent continuity component derived from the
original effect score. It prevents the repair from discarding the original benefit
ordering. It is not present in the modeling-ready dataset and is available only in the
legacy audit file.

Excluding the intercept, no baseline-risk component dominates: the legacy proxy accounts
for 24.7% of mean absolute linear contribution, current risk 16.1%, ED30 positivity 12.6%,
and clinical score 8.6%. All component contributions are in
`outcome_and_response_function_coefficients.csv`.

### Treatment response

Let

\[
\begin{aligned}
h(X)={}&-0.25+0.65z(LegacyBenefitProxy)+0.24CCM+0.18BH\ Risk\\
&+0.17Substance+0.13z(SDOH)+0.12z(Clinical)\\
&+0.10z(LowAdherence)+0.08z(Utilization)\\
&+0.18(CCM\times z(Utilization))+0.15(BH\ Risk\times z(SDOH))\\
&+0.10(z(Clinical)\times BH\ Risk).
\end{aligned}
\]

The individual relative risk reduction is bounded:

\[
r(X)=0.04+0.31expit(h(X)).
\]

No single component completely determines response. Excluding the intercept, the legacy
continuity component contributes 49.8% of mean absolute response-logit magnitude; the
remaining 50.2% is distributed across SDOH, clinical burden, adherence, utilization,
program, behavioral health, substance use, and three interactions. Exact contribution
percentages for every term are in the coefficient file.

### Treated risk and individual effect

\[
\mu_1(X)=\mu_0(X)\{1-r(X)\},
\]

\[
\tau(X)=\mu_0(X)-\mu_1(X)=\mu_0(X)r(X).
\]

This construction ensures `0 <= mu1 <= mu0 <= 1` and a nonnegative individual benefit.

### Observed outcome

For treatment `T`, factual risk is

\[
p_F(X,T)=(1-T)\mu_0(X)+T\mu_1(X).
\]

The observed outcome is a Bernoulli draw from `p_F`. The baseline-risk intercept was
calibrated to an expected 8% factual rate. Outcome seed 20360810 is the first accepted
seed in the documented search sequence that produced exactly 800 events and exceptions
in both ED30 directions.

## Per-patient causal fields

The revised dataset includes:

- `propensity_score`: repaired probability of treatment.
- `Propensity_Score`: identical compatibility alias for existing notebooks.
- `mu0`: probability of the ED outcome without treatment.
- `mu1`: probability of the ED outcome with treatment.
- `true_treatment_effect`: `mu0 - mu1`, an absolute risk reduction.
- `relative_risk_reduction`: `r(X)`.
- `factual_outcome_probability`: `(1-T)mu0 + Tmu1`.
- `dgp_version`, `dgp_seed`, and `outcome_draw_seed`: reproducibility metadata.

Ground-truth, audit, and legacy columns must be excluded from causal-model feature
matrices. At minimum exclude `Propensity_Score`, `propensity_score`, `mu0`, `mu1`,
`true_treatment_effect`, `relative_risk_reduction`, `factual_outcome_probability`, all
`dgp_*` fields, and all post-treatment intervention-process fields.

## Exact modifications and preserved components

Modified fields are limited to:

- dual eligibility;
- diabetes and CKD;
- depression, anxiety, substance use, and behavioral-health risk;
- the four SDOH indicators;
- clinical score, SDOH score, current risk, and risk tier;
- high-cost-drug and polypharmacy flags;
- propensity, outcome, and treatment effect.

All named binary prevalence counts remain exact. Clinical score, current risk, risk tier,
and outcome retain their complete marginal distributions. SDOH score retains its exact

All other original columns remain row-for-row unchanged, including:

- IDs and row order;
- demographic and geographic fields;
- program and case manager;
- utilization counts, cost, prescription count, and adherence;
- CHF, COPD, asthma, opioid, pregnancy, and living-alone flags;
- treatment assignment and all intervention-detail fields.

The complete column-level audit is `column_preservation_audit.csv`.

## Assumptions and limitations

- No external claims data were used to estimate epidemiologic correlation targets; the
  revised correlations are clinically structured engineering targets.
- Treatment is preserved row by row, so the repaired propensity is a calibrated target
  mechanism rather than proof of the unknown historical assignment code.
- The legacy benefit proxy is retained as a latent continuity component. It is documented
  and auditable, but should never be supplied to an HTE estimator.
- Pregnancy remains all `N`, and the client contract remains constant, because changing
  those marginals was outside the approved preservation-first scope.
- Existing post-treatment process fields are retained for compatibility but must be
  excluded from baseline causal models.
